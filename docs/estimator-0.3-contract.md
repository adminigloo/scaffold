Two builders work from this document at the same time, and neither can see
the other's code until the end:

- **Server builder** — `packages/estimator` 0.3.0 (sections 1–8, 11 and 12).
- **Widget builder** — `packages/estimator-widget` 0.2.0 (sections 8–9, 11 and 12).

Section 8.5 (the wire types) belongs to both of them. If you change a name or
shape there, the other half breaks without either builder noticing, so don't.
Section 3 (the recipes' field specs and explanation strings) is produced by the
server and rendered verbatim by the widget; the widget never re-derives a
number. If something here is ambiguous, pick the reading that best serves the
acceptance bar in section 0, leave a `// CONTRACT:` comment where you made the
call, and list it in your hand-off.

Section 10 (create-app) is a plan for a later step. Neither builder edits
`packages/create-app`.

## 0. The acceptance bar

The founder's bar, in his words: *"Business owners aren't devs. If a business
owner is told by their dev that it works, they deploy it and sell it to a
customer; they need to trust that it's actually doing what is needed."*

Estimator 0.3 is done when this scenario runs end to end, with no developer
filling gaps, for a wheelchair-ramp company (Summit Ramps, say). The test in
section 11.3 is this scenario, step by step:

1. **The owner sets up the price book** in a package-provided admin screen:
   products priced from their own supplies (cost per unit, quantity per unit
   of measure), labor (hours × rate, plus setup hours), waste, markup OR target
   margin, a minimum, the range rule, and options. Or they click **"Load a
   sample catalog"** on purpose. Nothing seeds itself from a public page.
   Business settings: name, contact, tax rate, currency, quote validity, lead
   recipients, follow-up delay, contingency, photos on/off and the AI budget.
2. **A customer gets an instant estimate** on the business's site (a page, or
   the embed on the business's own website). They answer what they know ("How
   many steps go up to the door?", and optionally the height of one step). The
   CODE turns that into the priced quantity and shows its working in plain
   words ("3 steps × 7–8 in each (estimated) = 21–24 in rise → ADA 1:12 slope →
   21–24 ft of ramp"). With a photo: the photo is shown back, the AI's
   **observations** are listed ("We counted 3 steps"), the count is pre-filled
   in the same stepper the customer can change, and the price updates live.
   Low confidence says so plainly.
3. **On save**, the photo (through the injected photo store), the AI reading
   and the customer's corrections are stored with the estimate. The business
   gets a **new-lead email** with the range, why it is a range, the reading,
   the corrections, the customer's details and a link to the estimate. The
   customer gets a copy.
4. **Staff open the estimate** in an admin queue (search, filter by status,
   archive) and see the photo, the AI reading beside the final measurement,
   and the priced lines. They **edit** it (add, remove or change lines,
   re-priced from the price book, totals recomputed in one transaction), then
   **send a formal quote**: the customer is emailed a link to `/quote/[token]`
   (noindex, no-referrer) showing the business's name, the lines, the total
   with tax, the validity date, and **Accept / Decline** with an optional note.
5. **Accept or decline notifies staff.** No answer → an **automatic follow-up**
   after N days, withdrawn the moment they answer. Quotes **expire** after
   their validity date.
6. **An accepted quote is one click from booking the visit** (scheduling 0.4's
   `VisitPicker` with `estimateId`) **and from becoming an invoice**
   (`@adminigloo/invoicing`). The package exposes the seams; create-app wires
   them when both features are selected (section 10).
7. **Public writes are protected**: honeypot + minimum fill time, per-key
   allowed origins, a rate-limit seam, the AI budget and per-requester limits.
   The widget takes the business's brand colour and follows the host page's
   light or dark theme. No forced "Powered by".

### The founder's rules (2026-10-12; non-negotiable)

- **A. The AI does no math, ever.** The model returns a tiny strict JSON of
  OBSERVATIONS per recipe (`{"seen":true,"risers":3,"riserHeightIn":{"low":7,"high":8},"confidence":"medium"}`).
  No measurements it worked out, no prices, no prose. Every number a customer
  or the business sees is computed by named, unit-tested functions (section 3),
  and every sentence that explains a number is generated by code from the
  values.
- **B. AI spend as small as possible.** Claude Haiku 4.5 (`"claude-haiku-4-5"`,
  $1 / $5 per million tokens) by default, configurable per install. The browser
  shrinks the photo to ≤ 768 px on the long edge before upload (≈ 600 image
  tokens); a few short prompt lines; structured outputs; `max_tokens` 150; no
  thinking; one call per photo; an identical photo for the same tenant and
  product never calls the AI twice; **no AI call when the customer types the
  measurement**; a per-tenant daily budget and per-requester limits that fail
  closed with "enter it by hand"; every call's tokens and cost recorded through
  an injected recorder. A small accuracy eval ships, documented and never run
  automatically (it spends money).
- **C. The range comes from real uncertainty, not a flat band.** Low and high
  are priced at the measurement's own low and high (steps 7–8 in → 21–24 ft →
  priced at 21 ft and at 24 ft; fixed items don't move), plus an optional
  owner contingency (default 0%). An exact measurement collapses the range to
  one number (± contingency). The 0.2 percentage band survives only as an
  opt-in per product. The admin says WHY a range is wide.
- **D. The price is built from the owner's supplies.** A supplies library
  (name, supplier cost, unit, supplier/SKU/note), quantity per unit of measure
  on each product, labor (hours per unit × rate, plus setup hours), waste %,
  markup % OR target margin %; a live cost breakdown and margin; a "test a
  quote" preview. Customers only ever see prices, never costs.
- **E. Customers answer what they know, never derived numbers.** Each recipe
  declares its customer inputs in plain words; the code derives the priced
  quantity. The ramp asks for the number of steps (required, a stepper) and,
  optionally, the height of one step (or the total height). Never "length of
  run". Without a height, the owner's assumed step height (default 7 in, range
  7–8 in) is used and said plainly next to the price. The photo only pre-fills
  the count (and a height range when the model is confident). "How many" is
  hidden unless the product makes sense in multiples, and then says what
  ("How many ramps?"). The owner configures each product's recipe, labels,
  help text and assumed step height, and sees a live preview of exactly what
  the customer will be asked.

## 1. What changes in 0.3, in one page

| Area | 0.2 | 0.3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What the customer is asked | the engine's measurement (width × height in inches, "length of run (ft)", "how many") | per-product **recipe** fields in plain words (steps, step height, width × length, count), from the server, with help text |
| Who does the arithmetic | the model, for photos ("rise → run at 1:12") | **code only**: named formulas per recipe; the model reports observations |
| Range | flat 90–115% band on everything | priced at the measurement's own low/high; exact → one number; contingency %; band is opt-in per product |
| Price | lumped per-ft/per-sq-ft rates + materials | **supplies library** × quantity per unit of measure (or per item / per job), labor hours × rate + setup, waste, markup OR margin; cost breakdown and margin |
| Options | flat modifier, marked up with the cost | flat **price** modifier, not marked up (the customer sees "+$120" and pays $120) |
| Photo | thrown away; model guessed sq ft / linear ft | stored with the estimate (photo store seam); reading stored; corrections stored; Haiku 4.5, ≤ 768 px, cached by hash, budgeted, metered |
| Business settings | tax rate in host code | `estimator_settings` row per tenant (name, contact, tax, currency, validity, follow-up, lead recipients, contingency, labor rate, photos, AI budget, brand colour) |
| After a lead | nothing (no email, no edit) | lead + copy emails; staff edit lines; send a quote by link; view/accept/decline; follow-up; expiry; revise; resend |
| Customer link | none | hashed quote tokens, several per estimate; `/quote/[token]` |
| Queue | newest 100 | search, status filters with counts, cursor paging, archive |
| Price-book writes | create + deactivate | update/retire/restore supplies (with affected products), attachments, options and values, products; reorder; sample catalog on request |
| HTTP | keyed embed only, CORS `*`, every failure "bad request" | one public factory (keyed embed AND same-origin), per-key allowed origins, buckets, spam checks, 500 for faults; an admin factory with an authorize seam |
| Events | `onLead` callback | one envelope, eight event types, after commit |
| Email | none | `@adminigloo/estimator/comms`: seven templates, link redaction, follow-up queue |
| UI | a modal (no tests) | `EstimateTool` (inline), button + modal, `QuoteView`; `./admin`: `EstimatorAdmin`, `PriceBook`, `EstimatesQueue`, `EstimateEditor`, `EmbedKeys`, `EstimatorSettings` |

Everything 0.2 exported still exists with the same signature, except the
items listed in section 12's "Breaking (0.x minor)": `buildTakeoffPrompt`,
`parseTakeoff`, `TakeoffResult`, `TAKEOFF_SYSTEM_PROMPT`'s text,
`EstimatorTakeoffRunner` / `runTakeoff`, and the embed's error body shape.
Where 0.3 adds behaviour to an existing function, it adds an options field.
Existing tests change only where section 12 lists a deliberate change, and
each such change is named in the hand-off. A 0.2 suite may move from the fake
to PGlite instead of teaching the fake new SQL; list it.

**The upgrade rule for columns.** Every new column is nullable or has a
default, so `drizzle-kit generate` produces plain `ALTER`s. Where a new column
changes behaviour, its DATABASE default reproduces 0.2 for rows written before
0.3 (or written by hand), and the 0.3 create schema (zod) sets the new
behaviour for rows written through the package. Example: `range_mode` is
`'percent'` in the database and `'measured'` in `createProductSchema`. Say so in
the column's comment.

## 3. Measurement recipes: where every number comes from (server builder)

A **recipe** turns what a customer can count or measure with a tape into the
quantities a price is built from. Each one is code: a config schema with
defaults, a customer-input schema, the field specs the widget renders, the
named formulas, and the sentences that explain the result. The widget never
computes anything; it renders the server's fields and the server's sentences.
`src/recipes.ts` (pure, no I/O), unit-tested formula by formula.

```ts
type RecipeKey = "ramp_over_steps" | "area" | "linear" | "count";
const RECIPE_KEYS: readonly RecipeKey[];
```

### 3.1 Three quantity sets, not one

Every derivation produces a **low**, a **point** and a **high** set of
quantities (rule C). An exact input gives three equal sets. The point is the
best single guess: what a lead is saved at in `measured` mode, and what a
quote charges.

```ts
type Quantities = Record<string, number>;   // keyed by quantity key, plus the legacy keys
interface QuantityRange { low: Quantities; point: Quantities; high: Quantities }
```

### 3.2 Quantity keys

| Recipe | Primary key (labor and "per unit of measure") | Other keys | Kind (for waste, 4.1) | Label in the admin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ramp_over_steps` | `rampFt` | `landings` | length / count | "ft of ramp" / "resting landing" |
| `area` | `sqFt` | `perimeterFt` | area / length | "sq ft" / "ft of edge" |
| `linear` | `linearFt` | — | length | "ft" |
| `count` | `item` | — | count | the config's noun ("doorway") |

Every set ALSO carries the legacy keys `sqFt`, `linearFt` and `units`, so a
0.2 attachment (`per` null) and the lump costs keep pricing as before:

| Recipe | `sqFt` | `linearFt` | `units` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ramp_over_steps` | 0 | = `rampFt` | 1 |
| `area` | = `sqFt` | = `perimeterFt` | 1 |
| `linear` | 0 | = `linearFt` | 1 |
| `count` | 0 | 0 | 1 |

(A staff line given a 0.2 `measurement.units` keeps that `units`, as 0.2 did.)

A supply attachment's `per` is one of the product recipe's quantity keys, or
`item` (once per item of the line quantity), or `job` (once per line).
Exported: `RECIPE_QUANTITY_KEYS: Record<RecipeKey, ReadonlyArray<{ key: string; kind: "length" | "area" | "count"; label: string }>>`
and `supplyBasesFor(recipe): string[]` (the keys, then `item`, then `job`).

**Default `per` for an attach that names none** (the 0.3 admin always names
one; this is for API callers): `per_linear_ft` → the recipe's length key
(`rampFt`, `perimeterFt`, `linearFt`; `item` for count), `per_sqft` → `sqFt`
(area only; any other recipe refuses `invalid_supply_basis`), `per_unit` →
`item`, `flat` → `job`.

**Changing a product's recipe** checks its active attachments: any whose
`per` is not a key of the new recipe (and not `item`/`job`/null) refuses
`invalid_supply_basis`, naming the supplies, so a price never silently loses a
line.

### 3.3 `ramp_over_steps`: "How many steps go up to the door?"

**Config** (`RampRecipeConfig`, wire 8.5) and defaults:

| Field | Default | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stepsLabel` | "How many steps go up to the door?" | 1–120 chars |
| `stepsHelp` | "Count every step up, including the last one onto the porch or landing." | ≤ 300, nullable |
| `stepHeightLabel` | "Height of one step (optional)" | 1–120 |
| `stepHeightHelp` | "Measure from the top of one step to the top of the next." | ≤ 300, nullable |
| `allowTotalRise` | `true` | |
| `totalRiseLabel` | "Or the total height, ground to door threshold (optional)" | 1–120 |
| `totalRiseHelp` | "If you measured the whole height instead, enter it here." | ≤ 300, nullable |
| `stepHeightIn` | `{ typical: 7, low: 7, high: 8 }` | each 3–12; low ≤ typical ≤ high |
| `slopeRatio` | `12` (ADA 1:12) | integer 8–20 |
| `roundUpToFt` | `1` | 0–10 (0 = exact) |
| `landingEveryRiseIn` | `30` (ADA: one run rises at most 30 in) | 12–60 |
| `maxSteps` | `20` | 1–30 |
| `photoSubject` | "the steps up to the door" | 1–80. Goes into the prompt (5.4). |
| `photoTip` | "Stand to the side so every step shows." | ≤ 120, nullable. Shown by the photo button. |
| `resultNoun` | `{ one: "ramp", many: "ramps" }` | 1–40 each |

**Customer inputs** (`CustomerInputs`): `steps` (integer 1–`maxSteps`),
`stepHeightIn` (3–12, optional), `totalRiseIn` (3–360, optional; only when
`allowTotalRise`). `steps` is required unless `totalRiseIn` is given. When both
a height and a total are given, the total wins.

**Fields** (`customerFieldsFor`; the order is the render order):

```ts
[
  { key: "steps", kind: "stepper", label: stepsLabel, help: stepsHelp, required: true,
    min: 1, max: maxSteps, presentation: "primary" },
  { key: "stepHeightIn", kind: "number", label: stepHeightLabel, help: stepHeightHelp, required: false,
    min: 3, max: 12, step: 0.25, unit: "in", placeholder: "e.g. 7", presentation: "primary" },
  // only when allowTotalRise:
  { key: "totalRiseIn", kind: "number", label: totalRiseLabel, help: totalRiseHelp, required: false,
    min: 3, max: 360, step: 0.25, unit: "in", placeholder: null, presentation: "secondary" },
]
```

No text a customer reads for a ramp — a field's label, help or placeholder,
an explanation line, the assumption, the result phrase, a reason, a reading
line or a message — says "length of run", "linear feet" or "run". The
scenario test (11.3) checks every customer-facing string the ramp's routes
return. (Enum values and keys such as `measurementMode: "linear"` or a
legacy `linearFt` are not text a customer reads, and stay for 0.1 widgets.)

**The formulas** (named exports, each unit-tested):

```ts
riseFromSteps(steps: number, stepHeightIn: number): number
  // steps × height, to 0.01
rampLengthFt(riseIn: number, slopeRatio: number, roundUpToFt: number): number
  // riseIn × slopeRatio / 12 ft (1:12 → 1 ft of ramp per inch of rise), then rounded UP to a
  // multiple of roundUpToFt (0 = no rounding), to 0.01. Snap before the ceil
  // (ceil(snap(x / step)) × step, snap = round to 1e-6) so 22.0000000001 never becomes 23.
restingLandings(riseIn: number, landingEveryRiseIn: number): number
  // max(0, ceil(snap(riseIn / every)) − 1): 30 in → 0, 30.25 → 1, 60 → 1, 61 → 2.
```

**Which step height.** In this order, the first that applies:

1. `totalRiseIn` given → the rise is exact (`low = point = high`). Basis `"total"`.
2. `stepHeightIn` given → the rise is exact, `riseFromSteps(steps, stepHeightIn)`.
   Basis `"measured"`.
3. A photo reading is attached (`takeoffId`, 5.5) with a usable
   `heightRangeIn` (confidence medium or high) → heights low/high from the
   reading, typical = their midpoint rounded to the nearest 0.25. Basis `"photo"`.
4. Otherwise the owner's `stepHeightIn` (typical / low / high). Basis `"assumed"`.

Then rise = `steps × hLow / hTypical / hHigh`; `rampFt` and `landings` from
each; `linearFt = rampFt`, `sqFt = 0`, `units = 1`.

**The sentences** (exact). `fmt(n)` = `n` rounded to 2 decimals, trailing zeros
dropped (7, 7.5, 7.25, 22.5). `fmtRange(a, b)` = `fmt(a)` when equal, else
`fmt(a)` + "–" + `fmt(b)` (an en dash, no spaces). "step"/"steps", "landing"/
"landings" and the result noun follow the number.

- Rise line, by basis:
  - assumed: `{steps} steps × {fmtRange(hLow,hHigh)} in each (estimated) = {fmtRange(riseLow,riseHigh)} in rise`
  - photo: `{steps} steps × {fmtRange(hLow,hHigh)} in each (from your photo) = {fmtRange(riseLow,riseHigh)} in rise`
  - measured: `{steps} steps × {fmt(h)} in = {fmt(rise)} in rise`
  - total: `{fmt(rise)} in total rise (measured)`
- Run line: slope 12 → `ADA 1:12 slope (1 ft of ramp per inch of rise) → {fmtRange(ftLow,ftHigh)} ft of ramp`;
  any other ratio r → `1:{r} slope → {fmtRange(ftLow,ftHigh)} ft of ramp`.
  Append ` (rounded up to whole feet)` (`roundUpToFt` 1) or
  ` (rounded up to {n}-ft sections)` (> 1) when rounding changed any of the
  three lengths.
- Landing line, only when `landingsHigh > 0`:
  `{fmtRange(landingsLow,landingsHigh)} resting landing{s}: ramps need one for every {every} in of rise`
  (`s` empty when the high is 1).
- Quantity line, only when the line quantity > 1: `× {q} {resultNoun.many}`.
- `assumption`: assumed → `Based on an estimated step height of {fmtRange(hLow,hHigh)} in. Measure one step and enter it for a tighter estimate.`;
  photo → `Based on a step height of about {fmtRange(hLow,hHigh)} in, from your photo. Measure one step and enter it for a tighter estimate.`;
  measured / total → `null`.
- `resultPhrase`: one length → `That's about a {fmt(ft)}-ft {resultNoun.one}.`;
  a range → `That's about a {fmtRange(ftLow,ftHigh)} ft {resultNoun.one}.`
- `reasons` (why the range is wide; empty when exact): assumed →
  `Step height {fmtRange(hLow,hHigh)} in (estimated)`; photo →
  `Step height {fmtRange(hLow,hHigh)} in (from the photo)`; and when
  `landingsLow !== landingsHigh` → `A resting landing may be needed (over {every} in of rise)`.

**Worked examples** (defaults; tests assert these strings exactly):

| Inputs | rise low / point / high | rampFt | landings | Sentences |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `{ steps: 3 }` | 21 / 21 / 24 | 21 / 21 / 24 | 0 / 0 / 0 | "3 steps × 7–8 in each (estimated) = 21–24 in rise"; "ADA 1:12 slope (1 ft of ramp per inch of rise) → 21–24 ft of ramp" |
| `{ steps: 3, stepHeightIn: 7.5 }` | 22.5 (×3) | 23 (×3) | 0 | "3 steps × 7.5 in = 22.5 in rise"; "ADA 1:12 slope (1 ft of ramp per inch of rise) → 23 ft of ramp (rounded up to whole feet)" |
| `{ steps: 3 }` + reading 7.5–8.5 in, medium | 22.5 / 24 / 25.5 | 23 / 24 / 26 | 0 | "3 steps × 7.5–8.5 in each (from your photo) = 22.5–25.5 in rise"; "ADA 1:12 slope (1 ft of ramp per inch of rise) → 23–26 ft of ramp (rounded up to whole feet)" |

### 3.4 `area`: width × length

**Config** (`AreaRecipeConfig`): `unit` (`"ft"`; a pre-0.3 area product
resolves to `"in"`, 0.2's inches), `widthLabel` ("Width"), `lengthLabel`
("Length"), `help` (null), `presets` (`[]`; ≤ 12 of

`maxLength` (1000 for ft, 12 000 for in; never above those),
`resultNoun` (`{ one: "area", many: "areas" }`).

**Inputs**: `width`, `length` (> 0, ≤ max, in `unit`). **Fields**: when there
are presets, a `choice` field `preset` first (`choices[i] = { value: String(i),

fields, which stay editable), then `width` and `length` (`kind: "number"`,
`unit`, `step` 0.5 for ft or 0.25 for in, required).

**Formulas**: `toFeet(value, unit)` (in → ÷ 12); `areaSqFt(w, l, unit)` =
ftW × ftL to 0.01; `perimeterFt(w, l, unit)` = 2 × (ftW + ftL) to 0.01. Always
exact (basis `"exact"`).

**Sentences**: `{fmt(w)} {u} × {fmt(l)} {u} = {fmt(sqFt)} sq ft` (`u` = ft or
in); `resultPhrase` `That's {fmt(sqFt)} sq ft.`; `assumption` null;
`reasons` [].

### 3.5 `linear`: a length the customer can measure

**Config** (`LinearRecipeConfig`): `unit` ("ft"), `lengthLabel` ("Length"),
`help` (null), `maxLength` (5000 ft / 60 000 in), `resultNoun`
(`{ one: "run", many: "runs" }` — only used in the quantity line; the owner
renames it, e.g. "fence"). **Input**: `length`. **Field**: one `number`.
**Formula**: `linearFt = toFeet(length, unit)`. **Sentence**:
`{fmt(length)} {u}`, plus ` = {fmt(ft)} ft` when the unit is inches.
`resultPhrase` null. Exact.

### 3.6 `count`: "How many?"

**Config** (`CountRecipeConfig`): `countLabel` ("How many?"), `help` (null),
`noun` (`{ one: "item", many: "items" }`), `maxCount` (50; 1–500),

**Input**: `count` (integer 1–`maxCount`). **Field**: one `stepper`. The count
IS the line quantity (per-item costs × count; per-job costs once). The

## 4. Pricing from the owner's supplies (server builder)

`src/build.ts`, pure. One engine prices the low, point and high quantity
sets and explains itself to staff. `calculateEstimatePrice` (0.2) stays
exported and unchanged except ES-18 (its low end is floored at the minimum),
marked `@deprecated — use buildPrice`.

### 4.1 The build, for one quantity set `q`

```ts
interface BuildSupply {
  assignmentId: string; supplyId: string; name: string;
  unitType: "per_linear_ft" | "per_sqft" | "per_unit" | "flat";
  unitCost: number;          // cents per supplier unit
  quantityMilli: number;     // thousandths, per `per`
  per: string | null;        // a quantity key, "item", "job", or null (0.2: scale by unitType)
  sortOrder: number;
}
interface BuildInput {
  quantities: QuantityRange;
  primaryKey: string;
  quantityKinds: Record<string, "length" | "area" | "count">;
  supplies: BuildSupply[];
  lump: { basePrice: number; pricePerSqFt: number | null; pricePerLinearFt: number | null; laborCost: number };
  labor: { hoursPerUnitMilli: number; setupHoursMilli: number; rateCents: number };
  wasteBp: number;
  pricing: { basis: "markup"; markupBp: number } | { basis: "margin"; marginBp: number };
  optionModifiers: number[]; // cents, PRICE per item
  quantity: number;          // items on the line (count recipe: the count)
  minimumCharge: number;
  range: { mode: "measured"; contingencyBp: number } | { mode: "percent"; lowBp: number; highBp: number };
}
buildPrice(input: BuildInput): PriceBuild
```

For one quantity set `q`:

1. **Item supplies** (once per item): each supply whose `per` is a quantity
   key (`unitCost × quantityMilli/1000 × q[per]`), `item`
   (`unitCost × quantityMilli/1000`), or null (0.2: `per_sqft` × `q.sqFt`,
   `per_linear_ft` × `q.linearFt`, `per_unit` × `q.units`, `flat` × 1).
2. **Lump costs** (0.2's price knobs, once per item): `basePrice +
   pricePerSqFt × q.sqFt + pricePerLinearFt × q.linearFt`.
3. **Job supplies** (`per = "job"`): `unitCost × quantityMilli/1000`, once.
4. **Waste** = `wasteBp` × (item supplies whose `per` is a `length` or `area`
   key, plus every null-`per` supply, plus the lumps) × `quantity`. Waste is the
   cut-off and spare on what is bought by the foot or square foot; it is not
   added to a permit, a delivery or a landing platform. (Null `per` and lumps
   keep 0.2's "waste on all materials".)
5. **Labor** = (`hoursPerUnitMilli/1000 × q[primaryKey] × rate + laborCost`) ×
   `quantity` + `setupHoursMilli/1000 × rate`. (For `count`, `q.item` = 1.)
6. **Cost** = (item supplies + lumps) × `quantity` + job supplies + waste + labor.
7. **Price before options**: markup → `cost × (1 + markupBp/10000)`; margin →
   `cost / (1 − marginBp/10000)`.
8. **Options** = Σ `optionModifiers` × `quantity`, added AFTER markup: an option
   value is a price the customer was shown.
9. **Line price** = `cents(max(price before options + options, minimumCharge))` (4.3).

### 4.2 The range (rule C)

`low`, `point`, `high` = the line price for `quantities.low / point / high`.

- **measured** (`c` = product `contingency_bp` ?? settings `contingency_bp`, ÷ 10 000):
  `estimateLow = max(minimumCharge, cents(low × (1 − c)))`,
  `estimateHigh = max(estimateLow, cents(high × (1 + c)))`. The saved line
  total is the **point** (contingency widens what is shown; it is not a price).
- **percent** (opt-in; 0.2's band): `estimateLow = max(minimumCharge,
  cents(low × lowBp/10000))`, `estimateHigh = max(estimateLow, cents(high ×
  highBp/10000))`. The saved line total is `midpointOf(estimateLow,
  estimateHigh)`, exactly 0.2's rule for these products.
- `single = estimateLow === estimateHigh`.
- Pricing's own reasons: contingency > 0 → `Contingency ±{pct(c)}`; percent
  mode → `Price band {pct(lowBp)}–{pct(highBp)} of the price (your setting)`.
  `pct(725)` = "7.25%", `pct(500)` = "5%".

### 4.3 Rounding

`cents(x) = Math.round(Math.round(x * 1e6) / 1e6)`: snap float noise to six
decimals, then round half up. `494547.5 × 1.4` is `692366.4999…` in floating
point; snapped it is `692366.5` and rounds to `692367`. Every price a person
reads is a `cents()`; intermediate sums are not rounded. Breakdown lines are
rounded for display and may differ from the rounded total by a cent; the total
comes from the exact values (the breakdown says so).

### 4.4 The cost breakdown (staff only)

```ts
interface PriceBuild {
  low: number; point: number; high: number;   // line prices, cents
  estimateLow: number; estimateHigh: number; single: boolean;
  lineTotal: number;                          // what a line is saved at (point; percent: the midpoint)
  cost: number;                               // cost at the point, cents(…)
  marginBp: number;                           // round((point − cost) / point × 10000); 0 when point is 0
  minimumApplied: boolean;                    // at the point
  reasons: string[];                          // pricing's own (4.2)
  breakdown: CostLine[];                      // at the point
}
interface CostLine {
  kind: "supply" | "lump" | "waste" | "labor" | "setup" | "markup" | "margin" | "option" | "minimum";
  label: string;           // "Aluminum ramp section", "Waste 5%", "Labor 7.35 h × $65.00"
  quantity: number | null; // 21, 42, 5.25, 7.35
  unit: string | null;     // "ft", "each", "h", "flat"
  unitCost: number | null; // cents
  amount: number;          // cents, rounded for display
}
```

Order: item supplies by `sort_order`, then job supplies, lumps, waste, labor,
setup, markup or margin, options, minimum. A `CostLine`, a cost and a margin
NEVER leave the server on a public route or the quote route, and are stripped
from admin responses for a caller without `estimator.costs.view` (6.12).

## 5. Photo reading: observations only, as cheap as possible (server builder)

`src/takeoff.ts` (pure), `src/image.ts` (pure), `src/takeoff-run.ts` (the
pipeline), `src/anthropic.ts` (the `./anthropic` entry). The 0.2 exports
`buildTakeoffPrompt(mode, context)`, `parseTakeoff`, `TakeoffResult` and the
old `TAKEOFF_SYSTEM_PROMPT` text asked the model for measurements; they are
REMOVED (rule A; "Breaking" in the changeset). The names `buildTakeoffPrompt`
and `TAKEOFF_SYSTEM_PROMPT` are reused with the new meaning below.

### 5.1 What one reading costs

| Part | Tokens (Haiku 4.5) |
| --- | --- |
| The photo, ≤ 768 px on the long edge (768 × 576 ≈ width × height / 750) | ≈ 590 |
| System line + prompt (5.4) | ≈ 100 |
| The output schema (structured outputs adds it to the request) | a few hundred at most |
| Output (one small JSON object) | ≈ 30–45; capped at `max_tokens` 150 |

At $1 / $5 per million tokens that is about **$0.001 per photo** (a tenth of a
cent). The default daily budget of $1.00 covers roughly 800 readings. The eval
(5.9) measures the real figure; the README quotes the measured number, not
this estimate.

**No prompt caching, on purpose.** Haiku 4.5 caches only a prefix of 4096
tokens or more; this request's fixed prefix is a few hundred, and the image
differs every call. A cache marker would save nothing. Leave it off.

### 5.2 The image rules

**In the browser** (estimator-widget, 9.4): accept `image/*`; decode
(`createImageBitmap`, falling back to an `<img>`); draw onto a canvas scaled so
the long edge is ≤ `photos.maxEdgePx` (768, from the config; never upscale);
export `image/jpeg` at `photos.jpegQuality` (0.8). A file that won't decode is
refused in the browser ("That file isn't a photo we can read. Try a JPEG or
PNG."). The resized JPEG is what is shown back, uploaded and read.

**On the server** (`inspectImage`, `src/image.ts`; never trust the client):

```ts
const PHOTO_LIMITS = {
  maxEdgePx: 768,          // what the browser targets (echoed in config.photos)
  acceptEdgePx: 1024,      // the server refuses a longer edge: a bypassed widget can't buy a 4,800-token photo
  maxBytes: 600_000,       // decoded bytes
  jpegQuality: 0.8,
  mediaTypes: ["image/jpeg", "image/png", "image/webp"],
} as const;

inspectImage(bytes: Uint8Array, declaredType: string):
  | { ok: true; mediaType: "image/jpeg" | "image/png" | "image/webp"; width: number; height: number }
  | { ok: false; reason: "not_an_image" | "unsupported_type" | "type_mismatch" | "too_large" | "too_many_pixels" | "unreadable_size" }
```

- The type comes from the magic bytes (JPEG `FF D8 FF`; PNG `89 50 4E 47 0D 0A 1A 0A`;
  WebP `RIFF????WEBP`). A declared type that disagrees is `type_mismatch`.
  GIF, HEIC and everything else are `unsupported_type`.
- Dimensions are read from the header: PNG IHDR (bytes 16–23); JPEG by walking
  the markers to the first SOF0–SOF15 other than C4, C8 and CC (height then
  width, big-endian); WebP `VP8 ` (14-bit fields at 26/28), `VP8L` (14-bit
  fields after the signature byte), `VP8X` (24-bit canvas width−1 / height−1).
  A header that can't be read is `unreadable_size`.
- `bytes.length > maxBytes` → `too_large`; a long edge > `acceptEdgePx` →
  `too_many_pixels`.
- base64 is decoded strictly (only the base64 alphabet, a length no greater
  than `ceil(maxBytes / 3) × 4 + 4`) before any of this.

Every refusal is the wire error `photo_invalid` (400) with the message "That
photo couldn't be used. Please try another one; the page shrinks photos before
sending." (`too_many_pixels` / `too_large` add "This one is too large.").

`sha256Hex(bytes)` (Web Crypto) is the photo's identity: the cache key and the
stored copy's `sha256`.

### 5.3 What the model may say: the observation schemas

Only recipes with `photoReading` have one: `ramp_over_steps`, and `count` when
its config names a `photoSubject`. `observationJsonSchema(recipe)` returns the
JSON schema sent as structured output (every object `additionalProperties:
false`, every property `required`, nullable values as `anyOf [..., null]`, no
numeric limits — the API doesn't take them; the server checks them):

```json
// ramp_over_steps
{
  "type": "object", "additionalProperties": false,
  "required": ["seen", "risers", "riserHeightIn", "confidence"],
  "properties": {
    "seen": { "type": "boolean" },
    "risers": { "anyOf": [{ "type": "integer" }, { "type": "null" }] },
    "riserHeightIn": { "anyOf": [
      { "type": "object", "additionalProperties": false, "required": ["low", "high"],
        "properties": { "low": { "type": "number" }, "high": { "type": "number" } } },
      { "type": "null" } ] },
    "confidence": { "type": "string", "enum": ["low", "medium", "high"] }
  }
}
// count
{
  "type": "object", "additionalProperties": false,
  "required": ["seen", "count", "confidence"],
  "properties": {
    "seen": { "type": "boolean" },
    "count": { "anyOf": [{ "type": "integer" }, { "type": "null" }] },
    "confidence": { "type": "string", "enum": ["low", "medium", "high"] }
  }
}
```

`normalizeObservation(recipe, raw: unknown): NormalizedObservation | null`
(zod; never throws; null = unusable):

```ts
type NormalizedObservation =
  | { recipe: "ramp_over_steps"; seen: boolean; risers: number | null;
      riserHeightIn: { low: number; high: number } | null; confidence: Confidence }
  | { recipe: "count"; seen: boolean; count: number | null; confidence: Confidence };
```

- Not an object, or `seen` not a boolean → null.
- `confidence` outside the enum → `"low"`.
- `risers` must be an integer 1–30, `count` 1–500; otherwise `null`. A
  `risers`/`count` of 0 or `seen: false` → `seen: false` and the count `null`.
- `riserHeightIn` must have finite `low ≤ high`, both 3–12, `high − low ≤ 3`;
  otherwise `null`. `seen: false` → `null`.
- Unknown keys are dropped. The stored observation is this normalized object,
  never the model's text.

### 5.5 From an observation to the form (code)


The lines (exact; `{s}` = plural "s" when ≠ 1):

| Case | Lines |
| --- | --- |
| ramp, read | `We counted {n} step{s}.` |
| ramp, height usable (medium/high) | then `Each step looks about {fmtRange(low,high)} in tall.` |
| low confidence | then `We're not sure about this photo, so please check the count.` |
| ramp/count, usable | last: `Change the count below if it's wrong.` |

| count, not seen or no count | `We couldn't count the {noun.many} in this photo. Enter the number below.` |
| read but more than the field takes | `We counted more than this form takes, so please enter the number below.` (usable false) |

**How a reading reaches the price.** The widget pre-fills `suggested` into the
same fields and sends `takeoffId` with every calculate and the submit. The
server re-loads the reading by `takeoffId` (this tenant's, this product's,
status `read`) and, for a ramp with no `stepHeightIn`/`totalRiseIn`, uses its
`heightRangeIn` when usable (3.3, basis `"photo"`). The client never sends a
range. A `takeoffId` that isn't this tenant's and product's is ignored on
calculate and refused `takeoff_unavailable` on submit.

**Corrections** (`correctionsFor(recipe, suggested, finalInputs): Correction[]`):
one entry per suggested key whose final value differs,
`{ field: "steps", label: "Steps", from: 3, to: 4 }`. Saved on the line.

### 5.6 The model call: `@adminigloo/estimator/anthropic`

```ts
interface TakeoffRates { inputMicrosPerMTok: number; outputMicrosPerMTok: number }
const DEFAULT_TAKEOFF_MODEL = "claude-haiku-4-5";
const TAKEOFF_MODEL_RATES: Readonly<Record<string, TakeoffRates>> = {
  "claude-haiku-4-5": { inputMicrosPerMTok: 1_000_000, outputMicrosPerMTok: 5_000_000 },
};
// both in the root barrel (pure data); ./anthropic re-exports them.

interface TakeoffModelRequest {
  system: string; prompt: string; schema: Record<string, unknown>;
  image: { base64: string; mediaType: "image/jpeg" | "image/png" | "image/webp" };
  maxTokens: number;
}
interface TakeoffModelResponse {
  output: unknown;            // the parsed JSON object; null on refusal, max_tokens, or text that isn't JSON
  inputTokens: number; outputTokens: number;
  stopReason: string | null;
}
interface TakeoffModel {
  model: string;              // the exact id called; recorded on every row
  rates: TakeoffRates;
  read(request: TakeoffModelRequest): Promise<TakeoffModelResponse>;   // may throw (network, timeout, 5xx)
}

// ./anthropic
type AnthropicMessagesClient = Pick<Anthropic, "messages">;   // type-only import of @anthropic-ai/sdk
interface AnthropicTakeoffOptions {
  client: AnthropicMessagesClient;   // the app's `new Anthropic()`; this package never reads a key
  model?: string;                    // default DEFAULT_TAKEOFF_MODEL
  rates?: TakeoffRates;              // required when `model` isn't in TAKEOFF_MODEL_RATES (throws at construction)
  timeoutMs?: number;                // default 20000
}
createAnthropicTakeoffRunner(options: AnthropicTakeoffOptions): TakeoffModel
```

The one call it makes (do not add parameters; the eval measures exactly this):

```ts
await client.messages.create(
  {
    model,
    max_tokens: request.maxTokens,          // 150
    temperature: 0,
    system: request.system,
    messages: [{
      role: "user",
      content: [
        { type: "image", source: { type: "base64", media_type: request.image.mediaType, data: request.image.base64 } },
        { type: "text", text: request.prompt },
      ],
    }],
    output_config: { format: { type: "json_schema", schema: request.schema } },
  },
  { timeout: timeoutMs, maxRetries: 1 },
);
```

- No `thinking` (Haiku 4.5 doesn't think unless asked), no tools, no
  `cache_control` (5.1), no streaming (150 tokens).
- `stop_reason` `"end_turn"` → `JSON.parse` the first text block (a parse
  failure → `output: null`); `"refusal"` or `"max_tokens"` → `output: null`.
- Usage from `response.usage.input_tokens` / `output_tokens`.
- The SDK's typed errors propagate; the pipeline catches them. No error, log
  line or row ever contains the API key or the image.

### 5.7 The pipeline: `runPhotoTakeoff`

```ts
runPhotoTakeoff(db, input: {
  tenantId: string; productId: string;
  imageBase64: string; mediaType: string;
  inputs?: CustomerInputs | null;      // what the customer has already typed
  requesterKey: string | null;
  now?: Date;
}, deps: {
  model?: TakeoffModel;
  photoStore?: EstimatorPhotoStore;
  recordUsage?: (usage: TakeoffUsage) => void | Promise<void>;
  maxTokens?: number;                  // default 150; 50–400
  settings?: EstimatorSettings;        // pass to skip the read
  onError?: (error: unknown, context: { tenantId: string; step: string }) => void;
}): Promise<TakeoffResponse>           // wire 8.5; refusals throw EstimatorInputError
```

Order of work. Each step that answers stops the pipeline.

1. Decode and `inspectImage` → refuse `photo_invalid`.
2. The product: this tenant's, active, shown in the estimator, estimatable →
   else refuse `product_unavailable`.
3. Settings: `instantEstimatesEnabled` false → refuse `estimates_closed`;
   `photosEnabled` false → refuse `photos_disabled`.
4. `sha256Hex(bytes)`.
5. **Keep a copy** when there is a `photoStore`: reuse this tenant's unlinked
   photo with the same `sha256` from the last 24 h, else `photoStore.put` and
   insert the row → `photoId`. A store failure goes to `onError` and the
   reading carries on with `photoId: null`: the reading never depends on
   storage.
6. **Already measured** (rule B): the ramp's `inputs` hold `steps` and a
   `stepHeightIn` or `totalRiseIn`, or the count's `inputs` hold `count` →
   `status "not_needed"`, no model call.
7. The recipe has no photo reading (area, linear, count without a subject) →
   `status "not_supported"`.
8. No `model`, or `aiDailyBudgetCents` or `aiPerRequesterDaily` is 0 →
   `status "unavailable"`.
9. **The cache**: a row for (tenant, product, hash) with status `read` or
   `unreadable` → that reading, `cached: true`. No limit is counted, no model
   is called, no usage is recorded.
10. **Per requester** (fail closed): rows created today (UTC) with this
    `requester_hash` ≥ `aiPerRequesterDaily` → `status "limited"`. A null
    requester key is its own bucket (`sha256(tenant + ":none")`). A failed
    count query → `"limited"`.
11. **The budget** (fail closed): Σ `cost_micros` of this tenant's rows created
    today + `maxTakeoffCallMicros(model, maxTokens)` > `aiDailyBudgetCents ×
    10 000` → `status "paused"`. A failed sum query → `"paused"`.
    `maxTakeoffCallMicros` = cost of 2 000 input tokens (a 1024-px photo, the
    prompt and the schema, rounded up) + `maxTokens` output tokens.
12. **Claim** (one model call per photo, even under a double tap):
    `INSERT … status 'pending' ON CONFLICT (tenant_id, product_id, photo_hash) DO NOTHING RETURNING id`.
    Nothing returned: a `pending` row younger than 60 s → `status "busy"`; a
    `failed` row or one pending for longer → reclaim it with
    `UPDATE … SET status = 'pending', created_at = $now WHERE id = $id AND (status = 'failed' OR (status = 'pending' AND created_at < $now − 60 s)) RETURNING id`
    (nothing returned → `"busy"`).
13. **Call** `model.read({ system: TAKEOFF_SYSTEM_PROMPT, prompt, schema, image, maxTokens })`.
    A throw → the row `failed`, `error` a fixed phrase ("timeout", "provider
    error"), `cost_micros = maxTakeoffCallMicros`, `cost_estimated = true`;
    usage recorded `errored`; `status "unavailable"`.
14. `normalizeObservation`; null, `seen: false` or no count → `unreadable`,
    else `read`. Store the observation, confidence, model, tokens,
    `cost_micros = takeoffCostMicros(usage, model.rates)` and `completed_at`.
    Record usage (contained: a throwing recorder goes to `onError`). Answer.

```ts
takeoffCostMicros(usage: { inputTokens: number; outputTokens: number }, rates: TakeoffRates): number
  // round((inputTokens × inputMicrosPerMTok + outputTokens × outputMicrosPerMTok) / 1_000_000)
interface TakeoffUsage {
  tenantId: string; takeoffId: string; operation: "estimator_takeoff";
  model: string; inputTokens: number; outputTokens: number;
  costMicros: number; costEstimated: boolean;
  outcome: "read" | "unreadable" | "errored"; durationMs: number;
}
```

The customer-facing messages per status (exact; `{things}` = "steps" for a
ramp, `noun.many` for a count):

| Status | `message` |
| --- | --- |
| `read`, `unreadable` | null (the reading's `lines` say it) |
| `not_needed` | "You've measured it already, so we've just kept your photo with your estimate." (no store: "You've measured it already, so we'll use your numbers.") |
| `not_supported` | "Thanks for the photo. This one is priced from your measurements, so enter them below." |
| `unavailable` | "We can't read photos right now. Count the {things} and enter them below." |
| `limited` | "You've used today's photo readings. Count the {things} and enter them below." |
| `paused` | "Photo reading is paused for today. Count the {things} and enter them below." |
| `busy` | "We're already reading this photo. Give it a moment and try again." |

In every status the stored copy's `photoId` comes back, so the photo still
reaches the business.

**Send.**

**View.**


- Opens the estimate; null for every miss.
- A `sent`/`viewed` quote whose `valid_until ≤ now` is expired first (the
  sweep's UPDATE for this one row), emitting `quote.expired`.
- The first view of a `sent` quote: `status = 'viewed'`, `viewed_at = now`
  (`UPDATE … WHERE status = 'sent'`, so two tabs make one move), emitting
  `quote.viewed` (notify false). A draft that was sent before reads

**Answer.**

```ts

  : Promise<PublicQuote | null>
```

| Found | Accept | Decline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| approved / converted | 200, as is, no event (a double tap) | 409 `already_answered` |

### 6.10 Embed keys and allowed origins

- `issueClientKey(db, { tenantId, label, allowedOrigins? })`;
  `updateClientKey(db, tenantId, keyId, { label?, allowedOrigins? })`;
  `listClientKeys` returns `allowedOrigins`; `revokeClientKey` unchanged.
- `normalizeOrigin(value): string | null` — `URL` parse; `https:` (or `http:`
  for `localhost`/`127.0.0.1`); no path, query or credentials; lowercased
  `scheme://host[:port]`. Invalid → a ZodError issue "Use the site's address,
  like https://summitramps.com".
- `originAllowed(allowedOrigins, origin: string | null): boolean` — an empty
  list allows any origin (0.2 keys); otherwise the request's `Origin` must be
  in the list. A keyed request with no `Origin` (a server, curl) is allowed;
  the allow-list is a guard on browsers, which always send it cross-site.
- `verifyClientKey` writes `last_used_at` only when it is null or older than
  10 minutes (ES-08: no write per keystroke). It returns the key's
  `allowedOrigins` too.

### 7.1 What each event sends

The customer email goes only when `data.notify` is true AND the estimate has
a `customerEmail`. Staff emails go to `settings.leadNotifyEmails`, or
`options.staffEmails` when that is empty, and never for a change staff made.

| Event | Customer | Staff | Follow-up queue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lead.received` | `estimate_lead_customer` (no link) | `estimate_lead_staff` | — |
| `quote.sent`, via `send` | `quote_sent` (with link) | — | withdraw, then queue |
| `quote.sent`, via `resend` | `quote_sent` (with link) | — | untouched |
| `quote.viewed` | — | — | — |
| `quote.accepted` | `quote_accepted_customer` (with link) | `quote_staff_notice` "Quote accepted" (customer actor only) | withdraw |
| `quote.declined` | — | `quote_staff_notice` "Quote declined" (customer actor only) | withdraw |
| `quote.expired` | `quote_expired_customer` | `quote_staff_notice` "Quote expired" | withdraw |
| `quote.revised`, `estimate.converted` | — | — | withdraw |

Scheduling's mechanics, unchanged: **withdraw first**
(`cancelScheduled(db, { tenantId, refType: ESTIMATOR_COMMS_REF_TYPE, refId: estimateId })`);
**each step isolated** in its own try/catch through `onError`; **with link** =
`issueQuoteToken` for this one message, send with `sendNow` and
`idempotencyKey: "estimate:" + estimateId + ":" + event.id + ":customer"`
(staff: `":staff:" + i`, sent side by side), then **redact** that one
`comms_messages` row (exact token → `:token` in subject and body);
**bounded** sends (`sendTimeoutMs`); **`ensureTemplates`** once per tenant per
process, `onConflictDoNothing` on `(tenant_id, key)`.

### 7.2 The follow-up

- Queued on `quote.sent` (via `send`) when: `followUps !== false`,
  `settings.followUpDays > 0`, the estimate has a `customerEmail`, and
  `sendAt = sentAt + followUpDays × 24 h` (then the last cron run at or before
  it, with `followUpRuns`) is before `validUntil` and more than an hour away.
- `enqueueMessage` fields: `templateKey: "quote_follow_up"`, `channel: "email"`,
  `sendAt`, `expiresAt: validUntil`, `refType: ESTIMATOR_COMMS_REF_TYPE`,
  `refId: estimateId`, `dedupeKey: "estimate:" + id + ":followup:" + quoteSequence`,
  `skipIfPast: false`, link-free `vars` (`quoteUrl` empty, `quoteLine` the
  fallback). A queued row sits in a table for days, so it holds no key.
- **`beforeSend(row)`**: null unless `row.refType === ESTIMATOR_COMMS_REF_TYPE`.
  Load `(row.tenantId, row.refId)`; skip with a reason when the estimate is
  gone, its status isn't `sent`/`viewed` ("the customer has answered" /
  "the quote is {status}"), the dedupe key's sequence isn't `quote_sequence`
  ("the quote was revised since this was queued"), `valid_until ≤ now`, or it
  has no email. Otherwise mint a token, remember `(row.id, token)`, and return
  `{ to: customerEmail, vars }` rebuilt from the estimate as it is now, with
  the link.
- **`redactSent()`**: scheduling's: exact replace for each remembered pair,
  then a sweep of the last 14 days of estimator-template rows for any
  43-character token (`(?<![A-Za-z0-9_-])[A-Za-z0-9_-]{43}(?![A-Za-z0-9_-])`).
  Returns the rows changed. (UUIDv7 ids are 36 characters with dashes and are
  never matched.)

## 8. HTTP handlers and the wire (server builder; 8.5 is BOTH builders')

### 8.1 Shared conventions

Both factories mirror scheduling's: one `handle(req)` routed by path suffix
(mount it at any base with a catch-all route); `Cache-Control: no-store` on

and use the wire's field names; an unexpected error is `500 internal` with a
fixed message, reported to `onError(error, { tenantId, route })` (ES-V3), never
its own text. Copy scheduling's `http.ts` into `src/http.ts` with the "copied;

Order of work for each request:

1. **OPTIONS** → 204. With an `Origin` header: `access-control-allow-origin`
   = that origin, `vary: Origin`, methods `GET, POST, OPTIONS`, headers
   `content-type, x-adminigloo-key`, max-age 86400. (A preflight carries no
   key, so origins are checked on the real request.)
2. **Who is asking.**
   - With `x-adminigloo-key`: `verifyClientKey` → unknown or revoked → 401
     `unauthorized` ("This estimator key isn't valid. Ask the business for a
     new one."). `originAllowed(key.allowedOrigins, Origin)` → else 403
     `origin_not_allowed` ("This estimator isn't set up for this website.").

     `Origin` (or `*` with none) and `vary: Origin`, so the widget can read
     its errors. No CSRF check: the key is the credential and no cookie rides.

     `unauthorized`; null → 404 `not_found`). Every POST must pass
     `isCrossSiteRequest` → 403 `forbidden`. No CORS headers.
3. **Route** → 404 `not_found`.
4. **`rateLimit(req, bucket, { tenantId, keyId })`** → false or a throw → 429
   `rate_limited` ("Too many requests. Please slow down and try again shortly.").
5. **Settings** read once; dispatch.

| `POST keys/:id/revoke` | settings.manage | — | `{ key }` |

Route details:

- **Every estimate mutation** answers the whole `EstimateDetail` again (so the
  editor never re-fetches) plus `warnings`.
- **`status`**: `StatusBody.status` is `approved` ("Customer said yes"),
  `rejected` ("Customer said no") or `draft` ("Reopen"); `notify` (default
  false) is passed to the event. Other statuses → 400 `invalid`.
- **`link`**: without `quoteUrl` → 404 `not_found`. Only for an estimate that
  has been sent (`sent_at` set) → else 409 `not_changeable` ("Send the quote
  first."). Mints a token → `{ url }`. It is how a business with no email set
  up (or wanting to text it themselves) gives the customer the link.
- **`convert`**: without `convertToInvoice` → 404 `not_found`; not approved →
  409 `transition_not_allowed` ("Only an accepted quote can become an
  invoice."); the seam's `EstimatorInputError` maps by code; anything else →
  500. Answers `{ invoice, estimate }`.
- **`estimates` list**: runs `expireQuotes` first when due (throttled), then
  `searchEstimates`.
- **`status` (GET)**: notices, in this order, each `{ code, message }`:
  `settings_not_saved` ("Set your business details, tax rate and quote
  rules in Settings."), `no_products` ("Add a product, or load the sample
  catalog, before you share your estimator."), `tax_default` ("Your tax rate
  is still the 8.25% default. Check it in Settings.") when the rate is 825 and
  settings were never saved, `emails_off` ("Customers won't get emails: email
  sending isn't set up.") from `delivery`, `photos_no_store` ("Photos can't be
  kept: no file storage is connected.") when photos are on and there is no
  store, `reading_off` ("Photo reading is off: no AI key is connected.") when
  photos are on and `photoReading` is false, `keys_without_origins`
  ("{n} website key{s} work on any website. Add your site's address to
  them.").

`EstimatorInputError` maps by its `code`; a ZodError → `invalid` with
`issues`; anything else → `internal`.

### 8.4 Wire field names

Server inputs ↔ wire: submit `name` ↔ `customerName`, `email` ↔
`customerEmail`, `phone` ↔ `customerPhone`, `address` ↔ `jobAddress`; staff
bodies use the server names. Money is integer cents; ratios are basis points;
instants are ISO strings; dates on the wire are instants (the widget formats
them in `PublicConfig`/settings' locale and zone).

### 9.2 Theme contract (every component)

- Props on every component: `theme?: "auto" | "light" | "dark"` (default
  auto), `nonce?`, `className?`, `style?`, `locale?` (overrides the config's).
- **Auto** resolves exactly as scheduling-widget's `resolveHostTheme` (copy
  it: `data-theme` on the nearest ancestor / body / html; `dark`/`light`
  class; computed `color-scheme`; the background the widget sits on; then the
  OS), re-resolving live (MutationObserver on html and body; `matchMedia`
  listener; cleaned up; StrictMode-safe). The resolved theme is written as
  `data-theme` on the root; before hydration the media query decides.
- **Tokens** are `--aiew-*`, declared under `:where(.aiew-root)` (zero
  specificity) with dark under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) { :where(.aiew-root:not([data-theme="light"])) {…} }`
  and `:where(.aiew-root[data-theme="dark"])`. Component rules are
  `aiew-`-prefixed at class specificity. Names: `ground surface ink muted
  faint line line-strong accent accent-strong accent-soft accent-ink on-accent
  ok ok-soft warn warn-soft danger danger-soft on-danger focus radius
  radius-sm shadow shadow-lg scrim font`. `accent-ink` is the accent used as
  TEXT (links, the selected chip's label). Light and dark values pass WCAG AA
  (a contrast unit test over `CONTRAST_PAIRS`, as scheduling-widget's).
- **The business's colour.** `accentColor?: string | null` on every component:
  undefined → `config.brandColor` (public) / settings' `brandColor` (admin);
  null → the stylesheet's tokens; a `#rrggbb` → that. Applied as inline
  custom properties on the root from the pure
  `brandTokens(hex, theme): Record<string, string>`: `accent` = hex;
  `on-accent` = `#ffffff` or `#0b0f13`, whichever contrasts more with hex;
  `accent-strong` = hex mixed 15% toward black (light) or white (dark);
  `accent-soft` = hex at 14% over the theme's surface (a solid hex);
  `accent-ink` = hex stepped darker (light) or lighter (dark) in 5% steps
  until it reaches 4.5:1 against the surface; `focus` = `accent-ink`. Tested:
  any colour yields AA pairs.
- **Injection**: `injectEstimatorStyles({ nonce })` → `<style id="aiew-styles">`
  once; `injectEstimatorAdminStyles({ nonce })` → `aiew-admin-styles` once;
  `estimatorWidgetCss()` / `estimatorAdminCss()` return the text. 0.1's
  `aie-*` stylesheet is gone (its invalid `font:` shorthand with it, ES-V4).
- **No "Powered by"** unless `poweredBy` is true (default false).

     mediaType, inputs })` where `inputs` holds only what the CUSTOMER typed.
     On `read`: the reading card beside the photo (stacked under 480 px) shows
     `reading.lines` and a confidence tag ("Not sure" for low); `suggested`

     disappears when the customer edits that field; `takeoffId` is kept for
     calculate and submit. Any other status: its `message`, and the photo is

   price stays visible, dimmed, while a new one loads.
   - `needs_inputs` → "Answer the questions above to see your price."
   - `priced` → the amount in whole currency units, large: one number when

     `assumption` in a highlighted note; when not single, "Why a range?
     {reasons joined with '; '}"; then "We'll confirm the final price once
     we've seen the job."
   - An error → "We couldn't update the price. Check your connection." (the
     form stays usable).
7. **Your details**: name, email, phone ("an email or a phone number so we can
   reach you"), address, notes; client checks mirror the server's; the
   honeypot `name="website"` off-screen (not `display:none`),
   `tabIndex={-1}`, `aria-hidden`, `autoComplete="off"`; `fillMs` from the

   rest with "Sending…". "Send me this estimate". Errors: `issues` onto the
   fields; `spam_suspected`, `rate_limited`, `network` → a message, the form

   them, then a message.
8. **Done**: "Thanks, {first name}. Your estimate {estimateNumber} is saved."
   `emailed` → "We've emailed you a copy." else "Keep this reference:
   {estimateNumber}." The range recap. With `bookHref`: a primary link "Book a

     → `quote_follow_up` with a working link; `redactSent()` leaves no token;
     then that customer declines with a note → "Quote declined" to the office.
   - **Expiry path**: a third estimate sent with `validDays: 5`; advance 6
     days; `POST admin expire` → `expired: 1`; that customer got
     `quote_expired_customer`, the office "Quote expired"; `accept` → 409
     `quote_expired` naming the business's phone.
6. **Book and invoice.** Pat's estimate detail lists `book_visit` (the
   `bookVisitUrl` seam's URL, with `estimate=`) and `convert`; `POST
   convert` calls the fake seam once (it runs `markEstimateConverted` in its
   transaction) → `converted`, the invoice link in the detail; a second
   convert → 409 `transition_not_allowed`.
7. **Protection, collected.** Every public POST of this file went through the
   rate-limit spy with the right bucket label; a cross-site same-origin POST →
   403; the model was called exactly as many times as distinct, read-worthy
   photos (asserted at the end).
